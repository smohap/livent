01

Vision & Positioning

Most event tools manage a single event: one date, one guest list, one RSVP form. Real events rarely work that way. A wedding is an engagement, a mehendi, a sangeet, a ceremony and a reception. A conference is registration, a keynote, breakout sessions and a dinner. Each has its own guests, schedule, budget and team — yet all of it belongs to one story, told to one host and one guest list.

Loom's core thesis: one event → multiple phases → multiple teams → multiple guests → one shared workspace. Rather than positioning as "an event planning app," Loom is positioned as the operating system underneath any event, of any size, for any purpose.

"Plan it. Invite. Coordinate. Pay. Experience. Remember."

Loom Product Tagline
02

Problem & Opportunity

Today, organisers stitch together five tools

  • A spreadsheet for the guest list and RSVPs
  • A separate invitation or e-vite tool per phase
  • A payments app or cash envelope for gifts and vendor payments
  • A group chat for coordination that guests and vendors both see
  • A shared drive for photos, contracts and floor plans

Guests experience the same fragmentation

A single wedding can generate five unconnected invitations, five RSVP links and five separate "which table am I on" conversations — for one celebration, one couple, one guest list.

Corporate organisers face a parallel problem at larger scale: registration, ticketing, speaker logistics, sponsor deliverables and post-event reporting typically live in different systems entirely.

03

Target Users & Personas

The Host / Owner

A couple, family or executive sponsor. Owns the master event, sets the vision and budget, and delegates execution to a manager and teams.

The Organiser / Manager

A planner, corporate event manager, or family member running day-to-day logistics — teams, vendors, budget, schedule and guest communications.

The Team Lead / Vendor

Owns one workstream — catering, photography, transport — with a scoped workspace, tasks and invoices, without visibility into the whole event.

The Guest

Receives one personalised invitation across every phase they're invited to, RSVPs, chooses a meal, sees their table, sends a gift, and shares photos — from a single simple dashboard.

The Sponsor / Exhibitor

A corporate partner managing a package, deliverables, booth logistics and invoices through a self-serve portal.

Check-in / Volunteer Staff

Front-line staff with a narrow, fast interface — scan a ticket, confirm a table, resolve a walk-in.

Killer Differentiators

01

The Multi-Phase Event Graph

One master event, unlimited connected phases — each with its own guests, RSVP, schedule and budget, rolled up into a single guest and organiser view.

02

An Event Operating System, Not a Reminder

Loom tracks who needs to do what, by when, with what budget, for which phase — not just the date and time of the event.

An Intelligent Seating Engine

Rules, constraints, dietary data and randomisation combine to auto-generate a seating chart in seconds — then let organisers refine it by hand.

Organiser Operations + Guest Experience, One Platform

Most tools optimise for either the planner or the attendee. Loom connects both sides of the same event in real time.

Information Architecture

Seven foundational objects underpin every feature: Master Event, Phase, People, Teams, Tasks, Money, Experience. Everything else — RSVP, seating, menus, tickets, sponsorships, media and schedules — attaches to these.

Organisation / Account └── Master Event (e.g. "Sarah & John's Wedding") ├── Phase — Engagement · Guests · RSVP · Schedule · Budget · Media ├── Phase — Mehendi · Guests · RSVP · Schedule · Budget · Media ├── Phase — Sangeet · Guests · RSVP · Seating · Menu · Media ├── Phase — Ceremony · Guests · RSVP · Seating · Menu · Media └── Phase — Reception · Guests · RSVP · Seating · Menu · Media

A guest sees one entry — "Sarah & John's Wedding" — with a per-phase status underneath (Attending / Maybe / Declined), rather than five disconnected invitations.

06

Core Feature Set

Eleven feature areas, each scoped with representative user stories and acceptance criteria. Tag indicates target release.

6.1 · Multi-Phase Events MVP

Organisers create a master event, then add named phases (e.g. Engagement, Mehendi, Wedding) from a template or from scratch. Each phase inherits master-event branding but carries its own date, venue, guest list, RSVP, schedule, budget and media.

  • As an organiser, I can add, rename, reorder and delete phases within a master event.
  • As an organiser, I can start from a Wedding, Conference, Birthday or Corporate Offsite template with pre-built phases.
  • As a guest, I see one event with a per-phase attendance summary, not separate events.
6.2 · Invitations & RSVP MVP

A drag-and-drop invitation builder supports personalised digital invitations sent by email, SMS, WhatsApp link or QR code, with per-guest or per-phase RSVP. Custom RSVP questions (plus-ones, dietary needs, accessibility, transport) are configurable per phase.

  • As an organiser, I can send one invitation covering several phases, and see per-phase responses in one table.
  • As a guest, I can RSVP "yes" to the wedding and "no" to the mehendi in a single flow.
  • As an organiser, I can configure automated reminders for guests who haven't responded by a deadline.
6.3 · Monetary Gifts & Sponsorship MVP → Phase 2

Guests can send a monetary gift, contribute to a group gift, or give to a nominated charity, with organiser-configurable privacy (public/anonymous amount and name). Corporate events add tiered sponsorship packages, a sponsor portal, contracts and invoice tracking. All payment processing routes through a licensed provider (e.g. Stripe/Adyen) — Loom never stores raw card data.

  • As a guest, I can send a gift with a message, and choose whether my name and amount are shown publicly.
  • As an organiser, I can define sponsorship tiers with benefits, and track each sponsor's payment and deliverable status.
  • As a sponsor, I can log into a self-serve portal to upload assets and see my invoices.
6.4 · Seating & Table Allocation MVP

For any phase requiring seating, organisers define table count and seats-per-table, then generate a randomised seating chart in one click — respecting "must sit together," "must not sit together" and VIP/children table rules. Organisers can regenerate, lock tables, or drag individual guests afterward.

  • As an organiser, I can set 20 tables of 10 and randomly allocate 200 confirmed guests in a single action.
  • As an organiser, I can lock a table so regeneration doesn't reshuffle it.
  • As a guest, I can see my table number and tablemates ahead of the event.
6.5 · Menu Sharing & Selection MVP

Organisers build a per-phase menu (starter, main, dessert, drinks) and, where selection is required, guests choose their meal and flag dietary restrictions during RSVP. Caterers see live aggregated counts by dish and dietary category.

  • As a guest, I can select my main course and flag a nut allergy when I RSVP.
  • As a caterer, I see a live count: 86 chicken, 104 vegetarian, 21 vegan, 13 gluten-free.
6.6 · Team Duties & Task Planning MVP

Organisers create teams (catering, decoration, photography, transport, etc.), assign tasks with owners, due dates, priority and dependencies, and each team gets a scoped workspace showing only what's relevant to them.

  • As an organiser, I can assign a task to the catering team with a due date and see it block a dependent finance task.
  • As a team lead, I only see my own team's tasks, budget and communications by default.
6.7 · Polls & Communication MVP

Event-wide or phase-specific polls (single choice, ranking, yes/no) alongside broadcast announcements to guests, teams, vendors or sponsors, delivered in-app, by push, email or SMS, with a high-priority emergency-broadcast mode.

  • As an organiser, I can poll guests on a date change and see results live.
  • As an organiser, I can send an emergency broadcast ("ceremony moved indoors") across every channel at once.
6.8 · Ticketing & Seat Selection Premium · Phase 2

For ticketed phases and conferences: multiple ticket types, promo codes, capacity and waitlist management, a visual seat map, QR-based check-in and a live registered/checked-in/no-show dashboard.

  • As an organiser, I can sell VIP, general and early-bird tickets with different prices and capacities.
  • As check-in staff, I can scan a QR code and confirm entry in under two seconds.
6.9 · Photo & Video Sharing MVP

A customised event media library with organiser-selected branding (background, frame, watermark), guest uploads into shared albums, and organiser-controlled download permissions (original vs. compressed, guest-only vs. public).

  • As an organiser, I can brand the gallery with our event colours and disable downloads until the reception ends.
  • As a guest, I can upload my own photos into the shared wedding album from my phone.
6.10 · Invoice & Expense Tracking MVP

Each team or vendor submits invoices against a budget category and phase; a lightweight approval workflow (team lead → event manager → finance) routes them to payment. Organisers see budget vs. committed vs. paid vs. outstanding in real time.

  • As a team lead, I can submit a caterer's invoice against the "Catering" budget line for approval.
  • As an organiser, I can see that catering is 18% over budget before signing off the next invoice.
6.11 · Event Details & Schedule MVP

A shared, phase-aware timeline with venue, map, dress code and responsible team per item, plus an optional live "run of show" view for the event-day control room, showing current, next and upcoming activities in real time.

  • As a guest, I see one shared schedule across all phases I'm attending, in my timezone.
  • As an organiser, on the day, I can see what's happening now and what's next across every phase running in parallel.

Non-Functional Requirements

Privacy & Security

  • Role-based access control and MFA for organisers and finance roles
  • Payment-provider tokenisation — no raw card data stored
  • Guest-level media and data-visibility controls, audit logs, data export and account deletion

Performance & Scale

  • Supports events from a 10-guest dinner to a 5,000-person conference on the same data model
  • Real-time RSVP, budget and check-in dashboards
  • Bulk operations (invite, remind, seat) complete for 2,000+ guests without blocking the UI

Accessibility & Localisation

  • Guest experience mobile-first, keyboard accessible, screen-reader compatible
  • Multi-language and multi-currency support per event

Platform

  • Organiser: responsive web + native mobile
  • Guest: mobile-first web/PWA at launch, no install required
  • Every event gets a shareable mini-website at events.loom.app/name

Risks & Open Questions

  • Payments & compliance — monetary gifting and sponsorship require jurisdiction-specific compliance (KYC, tax reporting) that varies by country; needs legal review before launch markets are finalised.
  • Complexity vs. onboarding — the breadth of the feature set risks overwhelming a first-time individual host; the MVP wizard and templates need to hide advanced modules until needed.
  • Seating engine edge cases — very large or highly constrained guest lists (e.g. many mutual exclusions) may not always produce a valid full assignment; needs a clear "couldn't fully seat N guests" fallback state.
  • Open question — should team/vendor accounts be free, or bundled into the organiser's plan? Affects vendor-side adoption incentives.
